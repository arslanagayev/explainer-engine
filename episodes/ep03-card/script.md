## Voice-over

What happens when you tap your card?
Here's the twist: your card has no battery.
The reader is a tiny radio station. It fills the air above it with a magnetic field.
Inside your card, a thin coil of wire runs around the edge. That's an antenna.
Bring it close, and the field powers the chip. Just enough, just for a moment.
That's why you have to get within a few centimetres.
Now the chip wakes up and talks back, over radio.
But if it only sent your card number, anyone could copy it.
So the chip holds a secret key that never leaves it.
It signs this exact payment: the amount, the shop, a random number from the reader.
The result is a one-time code. Useless for any other payment.
The terminal sends it through the card network to your bank.
Your bank checks the code with its copy of the key, and your balance.
Then it answers: approved.
The answer travels all the way back.
Beep. Green tick.
All of that, in about a second.
Now you know.
